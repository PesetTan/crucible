# License review — 2026-10-17

Crucible's original source, documentation, and original assets are GPL-3.0-or-later, as declared in the root README and package metadata; the complete GPLv3 text is in `LICENSE`. The existing Forge bridge already used GPL-3.0-or-later. Forge's checked-out source identifies itself as `forge-2.0.15`, and its source headers permit GPLv3 or later. Choosing the same license for Crucible avoids depending on an uncertain claim that its process boundary exempts it from Forge's copyleft.

This is a bounded repository and dependency-metadata review, not a legal clearance or a certification of previously distributed binaries. No past release artifact, recipient/source-access history, or third-party content permission was verified. Other repositories, including the separate Crucible Monitor repository and Pyre, are outside this license grant and review.

The initial live GitHub API check found [PesetTan/crucible](https://github.com/PesetTan/crucible) public, with no recognized repository license and no root license file before these changes. Its [releases collection](https://api.github.com/repos/PesetTan/crucible/releases) was empty. This confirms earlier public source distribution; it does not establish whether installers were shared elsewhere. See the private-repository decision below for the requested visibility change and its verification status. Committing or pushing this review does not change repository visibility.

## Private-repository decision

On 2026-10-17, the owner requested that `PesetTan/crucible` be made private while third-party content and data-use rights are reviewed. This is a precautionary distribution decision, not a finding that Crucible infringes copyright or that all uses of Magic card text are prohibited. The original-code GPL-3.0-or-later grant remains in place; repository visibility and software licensing are separate decisions.

**Visibility status:** a fresh GitHub API check still reports public visibility. The requested change reached GitHub's confirmation dialog, but automatic approval review blocked the next action and required fresh owner confirmation. The owner subsequently requested pushing the licensing changes and documented reasoning. The visibility change remains pending; do not treat private visibility as complete until GitHub confirms it.

The reasoning has two distinct parts:

- **Magic card text:** a read-only inspection of a temporary decompressed copy of `resources/starter/catalog.sqlite.gz` found 8,317 records with nonempty `cards.rules_text`. Crucible therefore bundles actual card wording, rather than only card identifiers, costs, or independently calculated simulation results. Wizards' [Fan Content Policy](https://company.wizards.com/en/legal/fancontentpolicy) does not establish blanket permission for this distribution: it excludes verbatim reposting and restricts use in games/game components. However, the [U.S. Copyright Office's games guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html) distinguishes unprotected game ideas and methods from sufficiently expressive text describing rules. Neither the presence of rules text nor the policy alone establishes infringement; the actual material, applicable law, permissions, and circumstances need assessment.
- **Untapped-derived data:** the starter manifest records 82 public deck snapshots and 1,548 associated deck-card rows, with dated source URLs and published statistics. Untapped's [Terms of Service, section 8](https://untapped.gg/legal/terms-of-service), expressly restrict copying, scraping, and redistribution of its service without prior written permission. This creates a separate contractual/data-use concern. Public visibility, attribution, and retaining a source URL do not themselves establish permission. These terms do not establish that Untapped owns copyright in every individual fact or that every deck list is independently copyrightable; enforceability and applicability are separate questions. No permission for Crucible's reuse has been verified.

Untapped's own display of Magic cards does not settle either question. Its [site footer](https://mtga.untapped.gg/) says it is unaffiliated with Wizards. No specific Wizards license or other legal basis for Untapped's card-content use was verified in this review. Unaffiliated status does not prove absence of a license, and the service's continued operation is not evidence that Crucible has equivalent rights.

Private visibility limits future public access through this repository. It does not recall earlier clones/downloads, resolve earlier distribution, grant permission to collect or reuse Untapped data, or remove license obligations when copies are supplied to others. No history rewrite or content deletion is part of this decision.

Before making the repository or packages public again, establish permission or another supported legal basis for the material being redistributed, or remove/replace that material and review the resulting distribution. Review Untapped-derived content separately from Wizards content. Complete the applicable dependency notices and matching-source arrangements described below. Removing a bundle or requiring a local database can reduce redistribution exposure, but is not itself clearance for every use of the simulator or its data sources.

## Current findings

1. **The missing project-wide license is addressed.** The root GPL license, explicit grant, and npm/Cargo identifiers now agree with the bridge. The macOS desktop, in-repository Monitor, and Windows packaging scripts now include `LICENSE`, `THIRD_PARTY.md`, and this review. Existing generated artifacts are unchanged.
2. **Complete binary notices remain unfinished.** `THIRD_PARTY.md` previously covered only some components and included the full SharpZipLib notice. It still does not contain all runtime/frontend/Rust copyright notices and license texts. Collect the applicable upstream notices for the actual shipped target, including .NET/Native AOT, SQLitePCLRaw, Svelte, Tauri, and transitive code. New package copies of these three documents are an improvement, not proof of full compliance.
3. **Matching source access must accompany distribution.** GPLv3 sections 4–6 require notices and a compliant way to obtain Corresponding Source, including the build/install scripts and applicable linked dependencies. For downloads, provide clear directions beside the binary to matching source at no extra charge and keep it available as required. A generic upstream homepage, or a private repository inaccessible to recipients, is insufficient. Direct recipient downloads of unmodified Forge/JDK archives differ from redistributing them; reassess if packaging changes. No source-release mechanism was added or release published in this change.
4. **Wizards and data-provider rights remain unresolved.** The software license cannot license material owned by Wizards or other sources. Wizards' [Fan Content Policy](https://company.wizards.com/en/legal/fancontentpolicy) requires an unofficial notice and restricts games/game components, verbatim reposting, trademarks, and commercial use. Therefore neither a disclaimer nor free access establishes permission for a simulator or the catalog. Confirm the applicable rights for card text, catalog data, screenshots, public deck lists, and statistics before distributing that material; remove or replace material whose redistribution rights cannot be established. No permission or infringement is asserted by this review.

For GPL-covered code, private use and private modification without conveying copies do not themselves require publishing the source (GPLv3 section 2). The existence of local ignored engine/runtime binaries does not by itself show a distribution violation. Conversely, a source repository's visibility alone cannot establish that previously supplied installers or browser bundles complied. See the [GPLv3 text, sections 2 and 4–6](https://github.com/Card-Forge/forge/blob/forge-2.0.15/LICENSE).

The conclusion is **no obvious software-license incompatibility found in the inspected metadata, with unresolved distribution and third-party content obligations**. Adding GPL does not retroactively cure omissions in an earlier distribution or grant rights over third-party content.
