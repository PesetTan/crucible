# Engagement Protocols for the Other Current Pilots

This is an inactive, opt-in candidate implementation and a practical evaluation of the installed `pilot-optimizer` skill. Standard remains on its existing pilots, including Reanimator v011. The existing Reanimator v012 preview definitions and adapters remain intact. No engine pointer, published optimizer policy, historical run or default assignment is replaced by this work.

## Declared sets and supported choices

The immutable Java catalog contains 34 entries: the 30 current subtype profiles, broad `aggro-v001`, broad `control-shared-v1`, the existing `reanimator-v012`, and an explicitly inherited `forge-default` baseline. `EngagementProtocols.REGISTRY` registers profiles against reusable typed definitions. The same immutable definitions drive the evaluator, candidate catalog introspection and frontend cards.

Broad Aggro receives Standard Execution and Close with Burn. Only the existing reviewed opponent-only burn adapter is changed: after Forge passes, it ranks legal burn by predicted damage reaching current public life, then predicted damage. Prediction does not adjudicate lethal or bypass prevention. A first revision also transferred subtype development into broad Aggro; its screening gain and regression caused rejection. The second revision removed that transfer.

The 30 subtype profiles retain their own frozen `subtypePilot.developOnPass.<exact-profile>` priorities and existing parent hooks. Their sets compose Standard Execution, Public Counterplay, Public Race and Board Presence. Aggro-parent subtypes additionally receive Close with Burn. These protocols rank only existing reviewed legal permanent choices after Forge passes. Counterplay prefers lower mana commitment when a supported observed counter route has available public basic-land mana. Public Race prefers a creature body against public combo evidence. Board Presence takes higher priority under the shared visible-pressure threshold. A body is not a prediction of a successful block. Own-main development does not implement a subtype's full strategy.

Broad Control uses the shared development set. Its optional development eligibility reuses positive reviewed annotations belonging to its explicitly registered Control subtype family. The existing sweeper-discard and graveyard-exile adapters retain their behavior. The candidate does not add general counterspell timing, broad removal targeting, flash sequencing, planeswalker loyalty logic, Fling/sacrifice execution, mulligans or combat policy.

Forge Default has only an informational Standard Execution declaration. It receives no new override. Many actions in every other profile also remain inherited Forge Default. Focused override fixtures cannot establish the full inherited Forge information boundary or expert play.

Paired baseline arms use each existing current pilot, including its inherited Forge paths. This batch does not add a separate all-Forge complete-game arm; Forge Default is the declared no-override reference and is checked as such in fixtures.

## Boundaries, state and provenance

New behavior requires `optimizerEnabled(player)` and a registered set. Historical profiles and ordinary runs keep their legacy branches. Unknown annotations and unsupported spell shapes remain inert. Casting eligibility still requires the existing mana-only permanent/burn shape, own accessible cards, legal timing, payment, phase, stack and legendary-duplicate guards. Permanent search retains the centralized 24-card bound; life, delay and commitment settings are centralized near `PROTOCOL_SETTINGS`.

The evaluator receives typed records without Forge objects. Observation adapters use public zones, observed casts, face-up cards and public basic-land mana; candidate adapters inspect the pilot's own offered choices. Opponent manifests, concealed hands/libraries and future outcomes do not enter the new policy. The public counter parser intentionally preserves v012's supported shapes. A typed restricted-route fixture checks that mixed creature/noncreature choices share one activation/rank vector without broadening that parser.

The new adapters add no per-card deferral or persistent game memory. Existing Reanimator deferral memory and reset logic remain unchanged. Existing per-game reset clears optimizer policies, protocol state, observations and turns; fresh controllers own their existing delay memory. Complete optimizer comparisons use fresh serial JVMs, so those games do not independently establish persistent-worker equivalence.

Optimizer experiments freeze exact deck versions/quantities/classifications, the same base annotations in both arms, opponent policies, seeds, starters, resources, source and fixtures. New protocol records include active IDs, version, policy identity, selected card and candidate ranks. Shared source-generated models support a profile-to-set catalog while old single-profile snapshots remain readable. New optimized saved sessions freeze the catalog; cache/session compatibility already compares policy, adapter and protocol hashes. Replay views resolve names from the recorded profile's definitions.

## Discovery and UI

Optimizer discovery now includes exact broad Aggro/Control assignments alongside subtype targets. It never matches hybrid strategy labels by substring and leaves opponent subtype policies unchanged. Source-run experiments continue to honor their recorded assignments. The replay evidence index is partitioned by both replay hash and exact profile, so inspecting one side cannot populate another pilot's packet.

The active frontend is Svelte. Forge Pilots offers an active-catalog / inactive-candidate selector and a protocol-set selector. Candidate definitions are generated by running `--protocol-catalog` on that candidate's compiled classes, saved and hashed beside its immutable source. Inspecting definitions does not activate a policy. The existing Blazor protocol components also understand profile sets, and both frontends retain saved-session/replay explanations.

The existing frontend and backend owners on 5077/5079 are retained. The modified Svelte frontend is served by an isolated UI-only preview on 5078. Desktop and 390px checks exercised Aggro, Control and the inherited Forge baseline; mobile uses one card column with no horizontal overflow. Candidate identity and inactive status remain visible. No second backend was launched.

## Evaluation process and evidence

The batch is closed after the user's reduced allowance. Sixteen profiles completed all ten screening pairs. Allies retained nine complete pairs before its technical timeout; the separate catalog-aware Aggro finalist retained two complete pairs before the requested stop. These are 171 matched pairs across separate final-revision experiments, with no measured outcome gains or regressions. Fifteen profiles did not reach screening. No holdout pairs completed, so no candidate is accepted or ready for promotion. All 32 targets have declarations and focused fixture coverage; that is not complete-game validation of all 32 profiles.

The final raw-evidence audit verified frozen requests, seeds, assignments, resource identities, candidate classes/catalogs and 247 selected-card casts. It found no nontechnical audit errors. The known Allies failure and the interrupted finalist attempt remain explicit. All 38 original saved runs, the active engine bundle and the Java runtime identity are unchanged. The backend reports zero active or queued optimizer jobs. The 667 new protocol checks, 64 existing Reanimator protocol checks, 43 Python checks, .NET identity checks and application builds passed.

The working evidence root is `data/reports/engagement-protocols-all-pilots/`. Its [REPORT.md](../data/reports/engagement-protocols-all-pilots/REPORT.md) and [coverage.json](../data/reports/engagement-protocols-all-pilots/coverage.json) give the exact per-profile completion state, deck/opponent identities, gains, regressions, changed state pairs and runtime ratios. Each experiment's `frozen.json`, candidate source/classes, fixture results, requests and hash-verified replays remain under `data/pilot-optimizations/<experiment-id>/`.

The skill's candidate and evidence limits are retained: at most 12 candidates and three code revisions per experiment, 10 screening pairs and 40 distinct untouched holdout pairs. This work uses two source revisions, instantiated for exact profile scopes. The initial shared allowance was 120 compute minutes, with a 6,800-second service allocation and a 400-second standalone preflight reserve. Screening stopped between jobs after 5,219.5 metered seconds to leave room for holdout. The user then reduced the total allowance to 90 minutes while the catalog-aware finalist was screening. The controller immediately paused it at 5,296.1 recorded optimizer seconds (88.3 minutes). That already exceeded the revised 5,000-second service allocation by 296.1 seconds. Preflight was not fully metered, so a total within 90 minutes cannot be certified. No further games are authorized by this batch. The controller now defaults to the reduced limit. Candidates with regressions or fixture failures are rejected. Any unfinished screen/holdout remains inconclusive. No candidate is promoted merely because its diagnostics pass or its finite outcomes tie.

Registered numeric priorities were inspected and retained. This task adds declared protocols and adapters, so the evaluated candidates are scoped code changes with focused fixtures and empty numeric tuning overlays; an automatic parameter sweep would not introduce the requested behavior.

Historical diagnosis covers all 32 targets. Fifteen profiles have a bounded saved-session replay example; the remaining 17 use the earlier subtype release's standalone diagnostic artifacts, explicitly marked as different historical resources/annotations. These examples identify supported decisions and coverage gaps; neither their frequency nor a loss establishes a mistake. Fresh paired baselines, rather than historical winners, determine comparison results.

The final focused suite has 667 checks across all 32 new targets, covering public context, concealed-hand/library/annotation invariance, actual engine choices and payment, legal fallback, absent metadata, phase/player guards, legacy behavior and reset. All 64 existing Reanimator protocol checks pass. The optimizer additionally runs its 335-position diagnostic inventory, required guards and legacy-change comparison for each evaluated profile. Eight known baseline diagnostic failures remain explicit; no new passed-fixture regression is permitted. Python optimizer checks cover broad-target discovery, unchanged opponents, profile-partitioned evidence, budgets, replay integrity and rejection rules. .NET checks cover assignments, policy/protocol cache isolation and old/new catalog serialization.

Comparative runtime includes fresh-process startup and actual game length. It is not a claim about pure computation speed. Early setup/build work shared the computer with screening, so small runtime ratios should not be interpreted as isolated performance measurements. No model is called during games or repeated computation.

## What the installed skill demonstrated

The CLI/API successfully froze and staged scoped source candidates, compiled real-engine fixtures, preserved base annotations and opponent policies, enforced complete paired outcomes, surfaced an equal-total-win candidate with a regression, and preserved an untouched holdout boundary. Rejection was useful: the broad Aggro transfer was removed in a bounded second revision instead of calling its equal win total an improvement.

Adaptation was needed for missing broad-profile discovery, profile-specific replay indexing, compiled-candidate catalog introspection, and a batch-wide computation reserve. The existing default screening schedule offers only five balanced matchup slots per profile; it cannot cover every broad Control variant/opponent combination in ten pairs. The optimizer also repeats baseline fixtures and fresh JVM startup across profile instances. That consumes meaningful budget and limits how many full per-profile holdouts fit in the allotted time. It has no automatic multi-profile acceptance result, so the report keeps individual coverage and outcomes separate.

The CLI has no supersede action for an unrun staged candidate. After verifying the involved experiments were idle, the existing local candidate-store method marked the first shared revision rejected before staging its successor. Pre-review records are preserved in `revision1-review.json`; sources, fixtures, completed games and frozen backends were not rewritten. This kept known superseded code out of later screening jobs.

After measuring screening cost, the unstarted profile queue was reordered to cover pending Control and combo/flash families before the batch reserve was reached. `execution-order.json` preserves the old and new queue order. This did not change any frozen per-experiment schedule, seed, opponent or alternating arm order. Execution order across distinct profile experiments is not an isolated runtime benchmark.

Publication review found that the previous optimizer publisher copied the baseline catalog even when candidate classes declared a different catalog. The current backend now copies the compiled candidate's verified catalog and validates an existing immutable bundle's catalog before smoke checks or pointer changes; regression checks cover this behavior. This batch's comparison backends were already frozen before that publication fix. Their evidence is retained, and the fixed entry point blocks their promotion where it would preserve a stale catalog. A catalog-aware experiment is required before activation. This limitation is separate from fixture/screen/holdout outcomes and is not bypassed by changing a frozen backend or setting an eligibility flag.

One separate catalog-aware Aggro finalist freezes the unchanged second-revision source and the corrected publication backend. Its exact decks, quantities, annotations, opponent policies and baseline bundle were checked equal to the earlier Aggro experiment; its recorded root seed is `20261016`. Its own screening and untouched holdout must pass independently within the same shared computation cap. It is recorded separately in `catalog-aware-finalist.json`; neither its seeds nor outcomes are silently substituted into the earlier comparison. Other profiles retain their individual completion states and publication limitation.

A source review found the mixed restricted-counter rank-vector issue before any holdout. The second revision uses one shared development context. This focused correction preserves the existing Reanimator parser and does not use a matchup tree.

Allies' second-revision screen encountered a 180-second candidate timeout in pair 9 against Dwarves. The partial replay ends at turn 36 after repeated inherited Earthbend activations; the trace is in Forge's removal/combat evaluation before its spell-selection call returns. Nine complete pairs and the unmatched completed baseline remain preserved. The timeout is a technical failure, never a rules loss, and blocks acceptance. `allies-technical-failure.json` records the exact request/replay reference and diagnostic tail. The budget and timeout are not extended to force a result.

Remaining unproven items include general play quality, broad matchup effects, full inherited Forge information safety, unsupported ability shapes, complete subtype strategy execution, persistent-worker comparative equivalence for these new sets, and any profile without a complete accepted holdout. Local diagnostic results are not Arena forecasts.
